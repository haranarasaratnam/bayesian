Section 2.2: Performance under Ideal Modelling Conditions

We first consider the performance of the Kalman filter under ideal modelling conditions, meaning that the system model is known precisely as are the process and noise models.

The key thing to note here is that the updated estimate \( \hat{x}_{k+1|k+1} \) always lies between the prediction \( \hat{x}_{k+1|k} \) and the measurement \( z_{k+1} \). Steady-State Performance

Figure 8 shows the predicted and estimated error covariances for position and velocity. In particular, note that they tend to constant values as \( k \) gets large.

Performance in the steady-state turns out to be dependent on the values chosen for the process and measurement noise covariance matrices, \( Q \) and \( R \).

Given that:

\[ P_{k+1|k} = F_k P_{k|k} F_k^T + Q_k \]

and:

\[ P_{k|k} = P_{k|k-1} - P_{k|k-1} H_k^T (H_k P_{k|k-1} H_k^T + R_k)^{-1} H_k P_{k|k-1} \]

We have:

\[ P_{k+1|k} = F_k [P_{k|k-1} - P_{k|k-1} H_k^T (H_k P_{k|k-1} H_k^T + R_k)^{-1} H_k P_{k|k-1}] F_k^T + Q_k \]

Section 2.3: Model Validation

So far, we have only considered the performance of a Kalman filter when both the system model and noise processes are known precisely. A Kalman filter may not perform correctly if there is either modelling or noise estimation error, or both.

Here, we discuss the causes and identify most of the important techniques used to control a Kalman filter from diverging. We consider two types of error and their characteristics:

  • Error in the process and observation noise specification.
  • Error in the modelling of system dynamics (process model).

The three tests that we introduced in the last section will be used as a basis for trying to tell when something has gone wrong with the filter.

Detecting Process and Observation Noise Errors

The example used in the previous section will be used to illustrate general characteristics observed when the process and observation noise are under- and over-estimated.

Matlab code is often used to simulate these scenarios and test performance under varying noise specifications. For example:

        N = 100;
        delT = 1;
        F = [1 delT; 0 1];
        H = [1 0];
        sigma2Q = 0.01;
        sigma2R = 0.1;
        Q = sigma2Q * [delT^3/3 delT^2/2; delT^2/2 delT];
        P = 10*Q;
        R = sigma2R * [1];

By comparing predictions and measurements, we can validate the correctness of noise assumptions and adjust them accordingly.

Measurement Noise Variance for Angular Velocity

1. Understanding the Encoder's Measurement Noise

A 12-bit encoder provides discrete measurements of the angle (θ) with a resolution determined by the total number of counts:

Resolution: \( \frac{360^\circ}{2^{12}} = \frac{360^\circ}{4096} \approx 0.0879^\circ \ (\text{per count}) \)

The quantization error introduced by the encoder is uniformly distributed over: \( [-\frac{\text{Resolution}}{2}, \frac{\text{Resolution}}{2}] \). The variance of this uniform distribution is:

\( \sigma_\theta^2 = \frac{\text{Resolution}^2}{12} \)

This variance represents the noise variance of the angular position measurements.

2. Relationship Between Angular Position and Velocity

Angular velocity (ω) is the time derivative of the angular position (θ):

\( \omega = \frac{d\theta}{dt} \)

For discrete measurements from the encoder, angular velocity is approximated as the finite difference:

\( \omega \approx \frac{\Delta \theta}{\Delta t} \)

where \( \Delta \theta = \theta_k - \theta_{k-1} \) is the difference between consecutive encoder measurements, and \( \Delta t \) is the sampling interval.

3. Noise Variance in Angular Velocity

The noise variance in ω depends on the noise variance in θ and the sampling interval \( \Delta t \). Assuming \( \Delta \theta \) noise is uncorrelated, the variance of ω can be approximated as:

\( \sigma_\omega^2 \approx \frac{2 \sigma_\theta^2}{(\Delta t)^2} \)

Substituting \( \sigma_\theta^2 = \frac{\text{Resolution}^2}{12} \):

\( \sigma_\omega^2 \approx \frac{2}{12} \cdot \frac{\text{Resolution}^2}{(\Delta t)^2} \)

4. Quantifying Measurement Noise Variance

Using the encoder resolution \( \text{Resolution} = 0.0879^\circ = 0.001535 \, \text{radians} \):

\( \sigma_\theta^2 = \frac{(0.001535)^2}{12} \approx 1.96 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{rad}^2 \)

For a sampling interval \( \Delta t = 0.01 \, \text{seconds} \):

\( \sigma_\omega^2 \approx \frac{2}{12} \cdot \frac{(0.001535)^2}{(0.01)^2} \approx 1.96 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{rad}^2/\text{s}^2 \)
