Interacting Multiple Model Estimator (IMM)

Overview

The IMM estimator is traditionally used in the field of multiple target tracking, especially when the system can be described by a bank of multiple state-space models. It has been shown to handle both maneuvering and non-maneuvering targets effectively. The algorithm consists of multiple interacting filters operating in parallel. The IMM estimator does not make a hard decision but assigns probabilities to modes. It efficiently handles systems with model mismatches and provides a powerful solution to dynamic systems with switching modes.

The Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) estimator is well-known within the tracking community for its adaptive estimation capability (Section 11.7.4, Estimation with Application to Tracking and Navigation by Bar-Shalom et al.). The objective of this note is to integrate the square-root formulation of a relatively new nonlinear Bayesian estimator called the Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF) with the IMM. The resulting estimator is called the CKF-IMM.

IMM Algorithm

The IMM algorithm is summarized as follows:

  1. Calculation of Mixing Probabilities: \[ \mu_{ij}(k-1 \mid k-1) = \frac{\pi_{ij} \mu_i(k-1)}{C_j}, \quad i, j = 1, \dots, q \] where: \[ C_j = \sum_{i=1}^{q} \pi_{ij} \mu_i(k-1) \]
  2. Mixing: Initial estimates are mixed using the mixing probabilities: \[ x_{0j}(k-1 \mid k-1) = \sum_{i=1}^{q} x_i(k-1 \mid k-1) \mu_{ij}(k-1 \mid k-1) \] Covariance is computed as: \[ P_{0j}(k-1 \mid k-1) = \sum_{i=1}^{q} \mu_{ij}(k-1 \mid k-1) \left[ P_i(k-1 \mid k-1) + \big(x_i(k-1 \mid k-1) - x_{0j}(k-1 \mid k-1)\big) \big(x_i(k-1 \mid k-1) - x_{0j}(k-1 \mid k-1)\big)^T \right] \]
  3. Mode-Matched Filtering: Each filter uses the mixed initial conditions and observations \( z(k) \) to update the estimates: \[ A_j(k) = p[z(k) \mid M_j(k), x_{0j}(k-1 \mid k-1), P_{0j}(k-1 \mid k-1)] \]
  4. Mode Probability Update: The mode probability is updated as: \[ \mu_j(k) = \frac{A_j(k) \mu_j(k)}{\sum_{j=1}^{q} A_j(k) \mu_j(k)} \]
  5. State Estimate and Covariance Combination: The final state estimate is a weighted sum of mode-conditioned estimates: \[ x(k \mid k) = \sum_{j=1}^{q} x_j(k \mid k) \mu_j(k) \] The covariance is given by: \[ P(k \mid k) = \sum_{j=1}^{q} \mu_j(k) \left[ P_j(k \mid k) + \big(x_j(k \mid k) - x(k \mid k)\big)\big(x_j(k \mid k) - x(k \mid k)\big)^T \right] \]

Applications

The IMM algorithm has been applied to:

  • Target tracking with glint noise
  • Speech signal enhancement
  • Localization in CDMA environments
  • Secure chaotic communication systems

CKF-IMM for Target Tracking

In this section we present the air traffic control tracking scenario., starting from [25,000 m, 10,000 m] at time \( t = 0 \, s \), the aircraft flies eastward for 125 s at 120 m/s, before executing a \( 1^\circ/s \) coordinated turn for 90 s. Then it flies northward for another 125 s, followed by a \( 3^\circ/s \) turn for 30 s. After the turn, it continues to fly eastward at constant velocity. The aircraft motion can be described by two different motion model segments—the (nearly) constant velocity model and the coordinated turn model.

Motion Models

The nearly constant velocity model is described by the following linear equation:

\[ x_{k+1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & T & 0 & 0 \\\\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\\\ 0 & 0 & 1 & T \\\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} x_k + \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} T^2 & 0 \\\\ T & 0 \\\\ 0 & \frac{1}{2} T^2 \\\\ 0 & T \end{bmatrix} v_k \]

The nonlinear coordinated-turn model is given by:

\[ x_{k+1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \frac{\sin(\omega T)}{\omega} & 0 & -\frac{1-\cos(\omega T)}{\omega} & 0 \\\\ 0 & \cos(\omega T) & 0 & -\sin(\omega T) & 0 \\\\ 0 & -\frac{1-\cos(\omega T)}{\omega} & 1 & \frac{\sin(\omega T)}{\omega} & 0 \\\\ 0 & \sin(\omega T) & 0 & \cos(\omega T) & 0 \\\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} x_k + \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} T^2 & 0 & 0 \\\\ T & 0 & 0 \\\\ 0 & \frac{1}{2} T^2 & 0 \\\\ 0 & T & 0 \\\\ 0 & 0 & T \end{bmatrix} v_k \]

Radar Measurement Model

The radar, stationed at \([20,000 \, \text{m}, 20,000 \, \text{m}]\), is assumed to measure range and bearing as:

\[ z_{k+1} = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{x_{1k+1}^2 + x_{3k+1}^2} \\\\ \tan^{-1} \frac{x_{3k+1}}{x_{1k+1}} \end{bmatrix} + w_k \]

The measurement noise covariance \( R \) is given by:

\[ R = \text{cov}(w_k) = \text{diag}([\sigma_r^2, \sigma_\theta^2]) \]

For the IMM-based approach, the initial mode probabilities are assumed to be \[ \mu(0) = [0.5, 0.5]^{T} \] and the mode switching probability matrix is given bu \[ \begin{bmatrix} \pi_{11} & \pi_{12}\\\\ \pi_{21} & \pi_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.95 & 0.05\\\\ 0.05 & 0.95 \end{bmatrix} \]
